I don't know what's going on - I always wanted to solve problems, create something and now that I have access to a tool that's able to speed up the whole design and development process... I just got bored.

I don't feel that passion of building anymore, since I'm not building at all. There's no building on letting something else build on my behalf, there's no thinking or design in prompting. There's no engineering or challenge. It's just "prompt", see the output, evaluate, adjust, repeat. What the hell is this?

I'm  always being a "maker" - of my kind. I studied topics in depth, spent hours and sleepless nights thinking about problems and solutions. I've experienced that great feeling of "this is a great idea" coming out of nowhere while doing unrelated activities, and having to rush back at my desk to write it down and implement it. That was great - it was rewarding, it was really meaningful. My brain was working and I felt satisfied every time I looked at what I created. Perhaps this is the result of 5 years of university studying computer engineering, but I guess not, it's something I've always done and the engineering mindset just fit.

During the years I developed software of any kind: from ML libraries to web development tools, passing through websites, wrote an infinite number of scripts for Linux system administration, set up servers, created online communities of tech enthusiast, designed database architectures.And a lot more - my guilty pleasure of mixing languages and technologies that I didn't know, just for the fun of experiment, learning, challenge myself, and spend some time having fun.

Now all of this is just... gone? I can just have an idea, ask the model of the month to implement it, and wow it's there - written in no time, and with the code quality that month by month I see increasing. If I start adding guardrails, rules, skills, and design documents before generating stuff, the model is going to generate pretty much the same thing I would have written (not really - but you got the idea). Of course everything depends on the size and complexity of the idea, but anyway, the possibility of prototyping in no time is nice... but terrible?

To be clear, no one is forcing me to do this on my free time. While on the job - and apparently in every corporate job - there's this huge pressure for using those tools to not get left behind... behind what? I'm quite sure that if someone finds a real productivity metric for the software development, that takes into account speed of execution, technical debt, maintainability, and cost - LLMs won't shine, and their introduction and usage without brain[^1] is just the generation of endless technical debt.

### What the point?

The point was never going from A to B - the point has always been to have fun while going from A to B, learn something new in the meantime, and gain more savvy.

That's the point - savvy. And I am losing it. From [a dictionary](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/savvy)

> savvy: having or showing perception, comprehension, or shrewdness especially in practical matters

Savvy is the fundamental idea for me - learning by doing. Do, make mistakes, learn from them, repeat, and grow.

With the LLMs every time there's a mistake, I don't learn. I can spot the mistake because I have some experience (and I'm lucky of being born 34 years ago), I can pin point the mistake and let the LLM fix it.

The shitty thing here is that LLM providers (but thanks to some good old Linux sysadmin skills I create my small LLM machine locally, I'll write about it sooner or later) can steal my savviness to train their models - so I'm not even going to see those mistakes anymore, and I can't keep my savviness trained.

### So... what?

I don't now - this blog post is mainly just a rant, humanly written, that's going to be used by some company to train some LLM and in the future used by someone else to generate something similar, when prompted "Write me an article that targets the niche of software engineers that are bored by the AI usage. Do not make mistakes".
